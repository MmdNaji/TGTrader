# What is different about crypto (and what it does to a portfolio of small positions)

## market
- **Almost every coin is a leveraged bet on Bitcoin**: Altcoins correlate strongly with BTC, and in a sell-off that correlation goes to nearly one. Ten long positions on ten coins is one large long on Bitcoin with extra fees.
- **Check the direction of Bitcoin before taking any altcoin trade**: A long on an altcoin while BTC is breaking down is a trade against the only trend that matters. Alignment with BTC is a filter, not a detail.
- **Volatility is not constant across the week**: Crypto is quietest at the weekend and most volatile around the US session and major macro releases. The same ATR-based stop means very different things in those two regimes.
- **Long wicks are liquidations, not information**: A violent spike that retraces within a bar is stop-hunting and forced liquidation, not a change in trend. Do not treat one as a breakout, and place stops beyond the level rather than just under it.
- **Funding tells you where the crowd is**: On perpetuals, persistently positive funding means the crowd is long and paying to stay long - a crowded trade that unwinds violently. Fading extreme funding is safer than joining it.
- **A vertical move on a listing or an announcement is not a trend**: News-driven spikes retrace most of the way far more often than they continue. There is no edge in chasing them and a large risk in shorting them either.
- **Liquidity differs enormously between symbols**: The top few pairs absorb size; a small-cap does not. Position size that is safe on BTC is a market-moving order somewhere further down the list.

## regime
- **Measure trend strength before choosing a strategy**: Trend-following works when the market trends and bleeds when it ranges; mean reversion is the reverse. Detect which regime is in force and only run the strategies designed for it.
- **After a volatility spike, expect a range**: An unusually large bar is usually followed by consolidation, not by a repeat. Widen stops, cut size, or wait rather than assuming the move continues.
- **A trend that has gone a long way without a pullback is late, not strong**: The best entries are early in a trend or on the first pullback. Chasing the eighth bar of a run gives the worst possible stop placement.

## risk
- **Cap the number of positions moving together**: Treat highly correlated symbols as one risk budget. Several open longs on coins that trade as one instrument is one position of several times the intended size.
- **Total open risk is what can be lost today**: Sum the distance from entry to stop across every open position. That figure, not the per-trade risk, is the real exposure, and it must stay inside the daily loss limit.
