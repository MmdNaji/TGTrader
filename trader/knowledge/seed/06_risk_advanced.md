# Advanced risk management (this is where accounts are saved or lost)

## risk
- **Risk a fixed small fraction per trade**: Never risk more than 1-2% of the account on a single trade. At 1% a losing streak of ten trades costs about 10%, which is fully recoverable; at 10% per trade it is ruin.
- **Think in R, not in currency**: Measure every trade as a multiple of its initial risk (R). A system with 40% wins and an average 2R winner is profitable. Judge trades by R, not by the dollar amount.
- **Cap total open risk**: The sum of risk across all open positions should not exceed a few percent of the account. Many small correct-sized trades can still add up to one oversized bet.
- **A daily loss limit is mandatory**: After losing a set fraction in a day (e.g. 3%), stop for the day. Most large blow-ups happen while trying to win back a bad day.
- **Reduce size after a losing streak, not increase it**: Adding size to recover losses (martingale) is the fastest path to ruin. If anything, cut size until the system is working again.
- **Position size comes from the stop, not from conviction**: Size = risk-amount / stop-distance. A tighter stop allows a larger position for the same risk; conviction never sets size.
- **Correlated positions share one risk budget**: Two longs on coins that move together are one trade for risk purposes. Do not let correlation quietly double your exposure.
- **Keep leverage low or off**: Leverage multiplies both the gain and the liquidation risk. For a learning system, spot or very low leverage keeps a bad run survivable.
- **Never risk money you need**: Only trade capital you can afford to lose entirely. This is not a cliché; it is what keeps decisions rational.
- **Protect profits with a trailing daily stop**: On a strongly green day, a rule that stops trading after giving back a third of the day's gains locks in a good day.

## psychology
- **The plan decides, not the feeling**: Every trade must match a written rule. A trade taken because it "feels right" with no rule behind it is the one to skip.
- **One trade is noise; the process is the signal**: No single trade matters. Follow the rules across a large sample and judge the system, not the last outcome.
- **Boredom is not a reason to trade**: The urge to "do something" when there is no setup is the most expensive habit. Flat is a valid, common, correct state.
- **After a big win, size stays the same**: Euphoria after a win leads to oversizing the next trade. The rules do not change because the last trade won.
- **Review the log, do not relive the trade**: Improvement comes from reviewing many decisions in the journal, not from replaying one loss. Every skipped setup is data too.
