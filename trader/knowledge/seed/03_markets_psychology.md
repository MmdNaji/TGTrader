# Market-specific knowledge and discipline

## market
- **Crypto weekends are thin**: Volume on Saturday and Sunday is much lower and moves are more erratic. Reduce size or skip new entries on weekends; hold existing positions with normal stops.
- **Funding rate extremes fade**: In perpetual futures, a very high positive funding rate means the crowd is long on leverage; sharp flushes down are more likely. Extreme negative funding is the mirror. Do not open a leveraged position in the direction the crowd is already crowded.
- **Bitcoin leads altcoins**: Most altcoins follow BTC's direction with more amplitude. Do not buy an altcoin breakout while BTC is breaking down; check BTC first.
- **Round numbers are magnets and barriers**: Price reacts around round levels (BTC 100,000; EURUSD 1.1000). Put stops beyond them rather than just inside them, and expect a pause on the first touch.
- **Forex sessions matter**: EUR and GBP pairs move during the London session (07:00-16:00 UTC) and the London-New York overlap; the Asian session is a range for them. Trend entries taken in the Asian session on EUR pairs usually fail.
- **Spread is a cost**: A spread wider than 10% of the stop distance makes the trade meaningfully worse. Trade liquid pairs and liquid hours.
- **Exchange risk is real**: Keep only the trading capital on an exchange, not savings. An exchange outage during a fast move is an unhedged position.
- **Liquidation cascades overshoot**: In crypto a cascade of forced liquidations pushes price far beyond fair value for minutes. Do not enter in the direction of a cascade; the best entries come after it stalls and the first bar closes against it.
- **Stablecoin and macro shocks**: A stablecoin losing its peg, a major exchange halting withdrawals, or a surprise rate decision invalidates every technical setup. On such news, close to flat and wait.

## psychology
- **Consistency beats brilliance**: A mediocre plan followed exactly outperforms a brilliant plan followed sometimes. The bot's job is to apply the rules the same way every time.
- **Revenge trading is a rule violation**: A trade opened within minutes of a loss on the same symbol with a bigger size is revenge, not analysis. After a loss, the next entry needs a fresh, complete setup.
- **Overtrading is the most common leak**: Most of the profit comes from a few trades a month. If more than three setups a day appear on the same symbol and timeframe, the filter is too loose.
- **Being flat is a position**: Not trading when there is no setup is a correct decision. Hold is the right answer most of the time.
- **Record every decision**: Every trade and every skipped setup gets a reason written down. Systems improve by reviewing the log, not by remembering.
- **Small money first**: A new rule runs at minimum size until it has produced at least 30 trades with positive expectancy. Only then is it worth normal size.
