# Market structure and regime

## regime
- **Classify the regime first**: Decide trend, range, or volatile before choosing a tactic. Trend-following in a range and mean-reversion in a trend are the two classic ways to lose. Use ADX (above ~22 = trend) and the slope of the moving averages.
- **Trend up: only longs; trend down: only shorts**: In a clear trend, take trades in the trend direction only. Counter-trend trades need a much higher bar and smaller size.
- **Range: fade the edges, not the middle**: In a range, buy near support and sell near resistance; never enter in the middle of a range where risk-reward is worst.
- **Stand aside in chop**: When ADX is low and price is crossing the moving average repeatedly, there is no edge. Not trading is a position.
- **Volatility expansion follows contraction**: Very tight, low-range bars (a squeeze) often precede a large move. Prepare for a breakout but wait for the direction to confirm rather than guessing.
- **Cut size when volatility is high**: When ATR relative to price is in the top decile of recent history, halve position size or stand aside; stops get hit by noise alone.

## market
- **Respect support and resistance**: Prior swing highs and lows, and round numbers, are where reactions happen. Enter near them with the trend, target the next one, and place stops beyond them, not just inside.
- **Higher highs and higher lows define an uptrend**: The trend is up only while price keeps making higher highs and higher lows. The first lower low is the first warning the trend is ending.
- **Volume confirms conviction**: Rising price on rising volume is healthy; rising price on falling volume is a warning the move is running out of buyers.
- **Gaps and spikes get faded**: A sharp spike with no follow-through usually retraces. Do not enter in the direction of an exhausted spike.
- **Correlation is hidden concentration**: Most crypto moves with Bitcoin. Three long altcoin positions are effectively one big leveraged bet on BTC. Count correlated positions as one when sizing risk.
- **Liquidity is best at session opens**: For forex, the London open and the London-New York overlap have the tightest spreads and cleanest moves; the Asian session is a range for EUR and GBP pairs.
- **Spread and fees are part of the edge**: A strategy that looks profitable before costs can be a loser after them. Only trade liquid markets where the spread is small relative to the stop distance.
