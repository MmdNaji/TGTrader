# Judging a system honestly: the arithmetic that decides whether to keep running it

## risk
- **Expectancy is the only number that matters**: Expectancy = (win rate x average win) - (loss rate x average loss), measured after costs. A positive expectancy with a 35% win rate is a good system; a negative one with an 80% win rate is not.
- **Know the drawdown arithmetic**: A 10% loss needs 11% to recover, a 50% loss needs 100%, and a 90% loss needs 900%. This asymmetry is why the size of the loss matters far more than the frequency of the win.
- **Thirty trades is the minimum before judging anything**: Under thirty results, a run of luck is indistinguishable from an edge. Do not change a system, raise its size, or abandon it on a sample too small to mean anything.
- **A losing streak of six is normal at a 50% win rate**: Streaks are expected, not evidence of a broken system. Decide in advance what evidence would actually mean the edge is gone, and use that instead of the last few trades.
- **Cut risk while the account is in drawdown**: Reducing size after a string of losses keeps the recovery arithmetic survivable. Raising it to catch up is the fastest known route to ruin.
- **Stop when the day's loss limit is hit, without exception**: Almost every catastrophic loss happens after a bad day, while trying to get it back. The limit exists precisely for the moment it feels wrong to obey.

## psychology
- **A rule that was not written before the trade is not a rule**: Any reason invented while a position is open is a rationalisation. If the exit condition is not defined at entry, the trade should not be open.
- **The worst trade to take is the one after a big win**: Confidence after a win produces oversized, unplanned entries. Size and criteria do not change because the last trade worked.
- **Judge decisions, not outcomes**: A correct decision can lose and a reckless one can win. Review whether the rules were followed; the result of a single trade carries almost no information.
- **Record why every trade was taken and skipped**: The skipped setups are half the data. A journal that only holds trades cannot tell you whether the filter is helping or costing.

## exit
- **Move the stop to break-even only after a real cushion**: Moving it too early converts winners into scratches, because normal noise is enough to return to entry. Wait for around one full R of profit first.
- **Trail a stop from the original risk, never from the last stop**: Measuring R from an already-trailed stop shrinks it on every step, so the stop walks into the price and closes every winner for almost nothing.
- **Take part of the position at the first target and let the rest run**: Banking a portion at 1R and trailing the remainder raises the win rate without giving up the occasional large winner that pays for the losses.
- **Exit when the reason for the trade is gone**: If the setup was a trend continuation and the trend has ended, the trade is over whether or not the stop has been touched. Waiting for the stop is paying for information you already have.
