# Entry and exit craft (established trading practice, not a secret edge)

## entry
- **Trade with the higher-timeframe trend**: Before any entry, check the trend one timeframe up. Only take longs when the higher timeframe is up or neutral, shorts when it is down or neutral. Fighting the higher timeframe is the most common way a good setup still loses.
- **Wait for the candle to close**: Enter on a closed bar that confirms the signal, not on an intrabar spike. Wicks reverse; closes commit.
- **Do not chase**: If price has already run more than one ATR past the trigger, the entry is late. Skip it and wait for the next setup rather than buying the top of a move.
- **Buy pullbacks in an uptrend, not breakouts into resistance**: In a clean uptrend, the higher-probability entry is a pullback to a rising moving average, not a breakout straight into an obvious resistance level.
- **Confluence over single signals**: A trade needs at least two independent reasons (e.g. trend + support, or breakout + volume). One indicator alone is noise.
- **Volume must confirm a breakout**: A breakout on below-average volume usually fails and returns into the range. Require volume above its recent average.
- **Avoid entries right before major news**: Scheduled high-impact events (rate decisions, CPI) spike spreads and whipsaw price. Do not open a new position in the minutes before them.
- **First pullback after a trend change is the highest quality**: The first pullback after a fresh breakout or trend change tends to work better than the third or fourth, when the move is tired.

## exit
- **Always define the exit before entering**: Know the stop and the target before the order goes in. An exit decided after the trade is open is an emotional decision, not a plan.
- **Take partial profit at the first target**: Scaling out part of the position at 1R-2R and moving the stop to break-even turns a good trade into a low-risk one and reduces the sting of reversals.
- **Move the stop to break-even after 1R**: Once a trade is one times its initial risk in profit, move the stop to entry so a winner cannot become a loser.
- **Trail behind structure, not by a fixed amount**: In a trend, trail the stop under the last higher low (long) or above the last lower high (short), so noise does not shake you out but a real reversal does.
- **Time stop for dead trades**: If a trade has not moved toward the target after a set number of bars, close it. Capital tied up in a stalled trade is capital not working.
- **Exit when the reason is gone**: If the setup was a trend continuation and the trend structure breaks, exit even if the stop has not been hit. The thesis is invalid.
- **Never widen a stop**: Moving a stop further away to avoid being stopped out is how a small loss becomes an account-ending one. The stop may only move toward profit, never away.
