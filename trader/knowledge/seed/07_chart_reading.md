# Reading the chart: where to enter, and how much to risk

## entry
- **Enter where risk is small, not where excitement is high**: The best entries are next to a level (support for a long, resistance for a short) where the stop can sit just beyond it. If the logical stop is far away, the entry is in the wrong place, however good the move looks.
- **Let the level come to you**: Mark support and resistance from prior swing highs and lows, then wait for price to reach one and react. Entering in the empty middle of the range is the worst risk-reward on the chart.
- **A retest is safer than the first break**: After price breaks a level, the higher-probability entry is the retest of that level from the other side (old resistance becoming support), with a tight stop below it.
- **Read the last three swings, not one candle**: Direction is set by the sequence of swing highs and lows, not by the colour of the current bar. Trade in the direction of that structure.
- **Momentum and location together**: A momentum signal in the middle of nowhere is a coin flip; the same signal at a support level with the trend is a setup. Require both a reason of direction and a reason of location.

## exit
- **The stop goes beyond the level, the target at the next level**: Place the stop where the idea is proven wrong (past the support/resistance that justified the entry), and the first target at the next opposing level, not at a random number.
- **Measure reward against risk before entering**: If the distance to the next level (reward) is not at least the distance to the stop (risk), skip the trade. A setup with less than 1:1 reward-to-risk is not worth taking.

## risk
- **How much to risk is set by the stop distance, then by the account**: First find where the stop must go (beyond the level). Then size so that if that stop is hit, the loss is your fixed small fraction of the account. Wide stop means small position; the risk in currency stays constant.
- **Fewer, better trades beat many mediocre ones**: A day with two high-quality setups taken well beats a day with twenty forced ones. Quantity of trades is not the goal; quality of location is.
